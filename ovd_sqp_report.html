Overview

Click-to-cart (14.55%) and CTR (1.56%) are relatively healthy; the primary challenge is closing sales once purchase intent has been shown.

Recommendations
CriticalFix Low CTR on High-Volume Generic Terms
Terms: "floor mats," "stair treads," "floor mat," "stair runner," "stairs" — 277,765 monthly searches at 1.27–1.49% CTR. Main images and titles aren't winning the visual fight on broad searches.
  • Main Image: Lifestyle shots showing treads installed on wooden stairs. White-background shots lose on broad terms.
  • Title Front-Loading: Move the strongest differentiator into the first 60 characters.
  • Bid Adjustment: Increase bids 30–50% to secure top-of-search placement. Premium pricing supports higher CPC if conversion follows.
CriticalAddress Cart Abandonment on Peel-and-Stick Products
Terms: "peel and stick floor tile" (9.26% conv), "peel and stick tile" (7.92% conv) — 96K and 48K clicks converting at half the rate of stair tread products.
  • A+ Content: Installation video modules and step-by-step visual guides. Reduce perceived DIY risk.
HighRescue Low Cart Rate on Specialty Terms
Terms: "bullnose carpet stair treads" (7.08%), "indoor rugs" (6.49%), "vinyl stair treads" (5.87%) — strong CTR, poor cart adds indicate detail pages don't match search intent.
  • Variation Architecture: Make bullnose a filterable variation, not buried in bullets.
  • Bullet Specificity: First bullet must address the material query directly.
  • A+ Comparison Module: "Carpet vs Vinyl vs Rubber" chart with clear use cases.
HighPrice-Segmented Catalog Strategy
The 87% → 367% premium gap from click to purchase is killing mid-funnel conversion. Budget-conscious clickers drop off when encountering the true price tier.
  • Entry-Level SKU: Value line at $79–99 targeting "stair treads" broad searches.
  • Tiered Campaigns: Separate SP campaigns for budget SKUs (<$100) vs premium (>$200) by keyword intent.
MediumDouble Down on Hidden Conversion Winners
Terms: "outdoor stair treads" (27.48%), "floor mat" (38.51%), "rubber stair treads" (26.70%) — punch above weight class. Every click is gold.
  • Dedicated ASINs: Standalone listings for outdoor and rubber variants.
  • Sponsored Brand Video: SBV campaigns for "outdoor stair treads" showing weather resistance.
  • Seasonal Deals: 15% Lightning Deals on rubber and outdoor SKUs during spring/summer.
MediumFix Bullnose Premium Pricing Disconnect
"Bullnose carpet stair treads" — clicking at $119.99, terrible cart rate (7.08%), weak conversion (9.06%). Premium price without premium perception.
  • Premium A+ Module: Cross-section diagrams of bullnose construction and durability testing.
  • Reposition for Professional Use: Title and position for "commercial stairs," "luxury homes," or "professional installation."
  • Remove from Broad Campaigns: Only bid exact-match bullnose terms. Stop wasting budget on generic traffic.

  • 01
    VisibilityBrand impression share contracted despite a stable category
    Total category impressions flat (33.9M → 33.6M) — demand held. Brand impressions dropped 23% (702K → 538K), share falling from 2.1% to 1.6%. At 2× the category price point, OVD doesn't need to own the category — but this rate of share loss on core keywords warrants attention.
  • 02
    ClicksBrand clicks followed impressions down — but CTR held
    Brand clicks fell 25% (22K → 16.5K), click share from 3.9% to 3.0%. CTR was essentially unchanged (3.14% → 3.06%), confirming the problem is visibility, not relevance. Shoppers who see OVD click at a consistent rate — they're just seeing it less.
  • 03
    ConversionCart-to-purchase conversion nearly doubled — the standout positive
    Cart-to-purchase improved from 4.2% to 6.0%. Premium buyers who committed in April followed through at a meaningfully higher rate. This is the metric that matters most for a high-ASP brand, and it moved in the right direction.
  • 04
    Mid-FunnelClick-to-cart slipped — listing content gap confirmed
    Brand click-to-cart dropped from 7.3% to 5.8%. Shoppers are finding the listings, clicking, then hesitating. At a $300+ price point this is expected friction — but the Prompts report already identified exactly what those hesitation points are: fade confidence, installation permanence, reinforced edge credibility.
  • 05
    BrandBranded search is strengthening — awareness is building
    The biggest impression share gainers are almost entirely branded: "oak valley designs stair treads" (+23.8pp), "oak valley stair tread" (+16.4pp), "oak valley brindle" (+11.8pp). Buyers are searching for OVD by name at increasing rates. For a premium brand this is the right trajectory.
  • 06
    CompetitiveCore differentiator keywords ceding ground to competitors
    Biggest losers are feature-specific: "35 bullnose carpet stair treads" (−17.2pp), "rounded bullnose carpet stair treads" (−16.4pp), "stair treads for wooden steps rounded" (−10.6pp). These are high-intent searches where OVD should be dominant. Losing share here to cheaper alternatives is the most actionable problem in this report.
  • 07
    VolumeThe category's highest-volume query is largely untapped
    "Stair treads for wooden steps" — 147K monthly searches, 89K brand impressions but 2.34% brand share and only 5 purchases. At 89K impressions already served, 5 purchases is leaving real revenue on the table regardless of positioning strategy.
  • 08
    Stability54% query churn between months is a structural concern
    539 new queries in April, 539 gone from March — out of 1,000 total rows each month. Only 461 matched across both periods. This level of volatility reflects either significant seasonal shift or inconsistent SERP presence. A brand with stable demand shouldn't be disappearing from half its tracked queries month to month.
  • 09
    OpportunityWool and bullnose signals growing — Nantucket has room to run
    "Wool carpet stair treads" jumped +10.7pp impression share (11.5% → 22.2%). Nantucket is the only wool product in the lineup and its listing currently has zero fade messaging — the #1 purchase driver in the category per the Prompts data. A fixable gap on a query moving in the right direction.
  • 10
    Funnel58 purchases from 537K brand impressions tells the real story
    Full funnel: Impr → Click (3.1%) → Cart (5.8%) → Purchase (6.0%). For a brand at 2× category price, low purchase volume per impression is acceptable — what's not acceptable is that the cart-to-purchase rate, now improving, was historically so weak. The April recovery suggests buyers are more qualified. Priority: protect impression share on branded and feature terms so that qualified traffic keeps arriving.
